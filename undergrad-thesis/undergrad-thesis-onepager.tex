%============================================================== COLUMN 1 ======
\head{What a thesis is}
A supervised project, 4--9 months, that shows you can take a research-level
problem, work methodically, and explain the result.
\sub{Examiners look for}a clear question\dt correct methodology (sane
baselines, honest evaluation, no leakage)\dt \textbf{understanding} --- you
must explain every design decision and every line you submit\dt clear
writing\dt honest limitations.
\sub{Not required}beating the state of the art, a new architecture, or
training a big model from scratch. A careful reproduction with analysis is a
good thesis; a record chase ending in one unexplained number is not.
\begin{flag}
\textbf{Scope test --- the usual failure.} Can you name the \textbf{dataset}
and \textbf{metric} today? Can you run \textbf{one baseline} within
\textbf{two weeks}? If your main idea fails completely, is there still a
thesis? Any ``no'' $=$ narrow it with your supervisor before writing code.
\end{flag}

\head{Finding a topic: three routes}
You do not need an original idea --- you need a well-defined problem with data
and a way to measure success. \textbf{Check advertised topics first:} most
supervisors list open ones on their page or the department board. Pick one and
do a small piece of research before the first meeting (key papers, dataset,
metric, three questions). Only if you are free to choose, use the routes below.
\sub{A. Shared task / challenge}Enter an organised competition: data, metric,
baseline, leaderboard and deadline already exist. \textbf{The strongest
default.}
\sub{B. Application thesis}Take a method that works to a new language (Greek),
domain (legal, medical, financial, code), or constraint (runs on a laptop,
$<$100\,ms, 50 labels, no cloud API).
\sub{C. Analysis / theory}Reproduction and stress-testing\dt ablation: which of
the five components actually matters\dt robustness (typos, dialect, domain
shift, long inputs)\dt probing and interpretability\dt efficiency
(distillation, quantisation, pruning)\dt critique of the metric with a small
human evaluation\dt a small, complete theoretical result.

\head{Why a shared task fits so well}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The hardest parts --- problem definition, data, annotation, evaluation
        protocol --- are settled.
  \item A hidden test set means you cannot fool yourself.
  \item Organisers publish a baseline; past editions give a whole comparison
        table free.
  \item A deadline: the best project-management tool ever invented.
  \item A \textbf{system description paper} is peer-reviewed, archival and
        achievable --- often your first paper.
  \item ``4th of 31 teams'' is concrete on a CV.
\end{itemize}
\sub{Where to find them}\lnk{https://semeval.github.io}{SemEval} (dozens of
tasks a year)\dt workshop shared tasks at *ACL venues --- WMT, CoNLL, BabyLM,
BioNLP, WNUT, CheckThat! (see \lnk{https://aclanthology.org}{ACL Anthology} +
each conference's workshop list)\dt
\lnk{https://www.clef-initiative.eu}{CLEF} labs and
\lnk{https://trec.nist.gov}{TREC} tracks (BioASQ)\dt NeurIPS competition
track\dt \lnk{https://www.kaggle.com}{Kaggle},
\lnk{https://www.codabench.org}{CodaBench},
\lnk{https://eval.ai}{EvalAI}, \lnk{https://www.aicrowd.com}{AIcrowd},
\lnk{https://www.drivendata.org}{DrivenData}\dt
\lnk{https://huggingface.co}{Hugging Face} leaderboards.
\begin{flag}
\textbf{Check the dates first.} Registration $\to$ data release $\to$ dev phase
$\to$ a short evaluation window $\to$ system paper. Dates change every edition:
read the task page and confirm they fit your thesis deadline.
\textbf{Window closed?} Run it retrospectively --- past data, metrics and the
published participant table are usually public.
\end{flag}
\sub{Running it as a thesis}Read last year's \emph{task overview} + 3--4 system
papers (30 min each)\dt reproduce the organisers' baseline \emph{first}\dt
build your own validation split and trust it\dt log every run\dt submit
something weak early --- pipelines always break\dt the thesis is the long
version of the system paper.

%============================================================== COLUMN 2 ======
\head{Where gaps are written down}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Limitations sections} --- mandatory at ACL venues: authors list
        what their method cannot do. A topic menu.
  \item \textbf{Future work} in recent papers and surveys.
  \item \textbf{Survey taxonomies} --- find the boxes with one or two entries.
  \item \textbf{Workshop calls for papers} --- this year's open problems, in
        bullet form.
  \item \textbf{Task overview papers} --- the closing section says what nobody
        solved.
  \item \textbf{Plateaued leaderboards}, or a simple baseline suspiciously
        close to the top.
\end{enumerate}
Also: GitHub issues (``cannot reproduce Table 2'', ``does not work for language
X'') and failed reproducibility reports.

\head{The one-page proposal}
Write it before any code; send it to your supervisor.
\textbf{Question} (one sentence, with a verb)\dt \textbf{why it matters} (two
sentences, no hype)\dt \textbf{data} (name, size, licence)\dt \textbf{metric}
(official + what else you report)\dt \textbf{baselines} (trivial + strong)\dt
\textbf{method} (three bullets)\dt \textbf{compute} (and whether it
suffices)\dt \textbf{milestones} (baseline, first result, freeze, draft)\dt
\textbf{risks + fallback} (the thesis you still have if it fails).

\head{Toolkit, week one}
\sub{References}\lnk{https://www.zotero.org}{Zotero} + connector +
\lnk{https://retorque.re/zotero-better-bibtex/}{Better BibTeX}: pinned keys
(\code{devlin2019bert}), auto-export into your thesis \code{refs.bib}. One-line
note on every save\dt Zotero flags retracted items\dt
\lnk{https://unpaywall.org/products/extension}{Unpaywall} finds free copies of
paywalled PDFs.
\sub{Fastest \code{.bib} entry}Scholar \emph{Settings} $\to$
\emph{Bibliography manager} $\to$ show BibTeX links; then \textbf{Cite} $\to$
\textbf{BibTeX} $\to$ paste into \code{refs.bib} (or Zotero \emph{Import from
clipboard}); bulk-export from \emph{My library}. \textbf{Check it} --- ``arXiv
preprint'' for a published paper is the usual error. Cleaner:
\lnk{https://aclanthology.org}{Anthology} / \lnk{https://dblp.org}{DBLP}
per-paper BibTeX, or any DOI from the terminal:
\code{curl -LH "Accept: application/x-bibtex" https://doi.org/<DOI>}. Always
cite the \textbf{published} version\dt protect capitals (\code{\{BERT\}})\dt run
\code{bibtex-tidy} and check every DOI resolves before submitting.
\sub{Writing}\lnk{https://www.overleaf.com}{Overleaf} (co-editing + comments
with your supervisor, a metered AI assistant; check for a university
licence)\dt \lnk{https://prism.openai.com}{Prism} \textbf{closes 1 Jan 2027}
--- do not start there; export now\dt locally \code{git} + \code{latexmk -pdf -pvc}, \textbf{one sentence
per line}, \code{\textbackslash citet} vs \code{\textbackslash citep}\dt start
from your department's template.
\sub{Code \& compute}one git repo for everything, commit daily\dt free GPUs:
Colab, Kaggle Notebooks (longer sessions)\dt ask about lab machines in week one
--- accounts take time\dt track runs (W\&B/MLflow) with config, metrics, commit
hash\dt check model/dataset \textbf{licences}\dt personal data $\Rightarrow$
talk to your supervisor before collecting (GDPR is not optional).

\head{Search \& judge the literature}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{2.1cm}@{\hspace{3pt}}X@{}}
\rowcolor{rowtint}\code{"exact phrase"} & quote technical terms \\
\code{a OR b} & must be capitalised \\
\rowcolor{rowtint}\code{-survey} & exclude a term \\
\code{source:"SemEval"} & search inside a venue or task \\
\rowcolor{rowtint}\code{intitle:x} & precision filter \\
\textbf{Cited by} $\to$ \textbf{search within citing articles} & who applied it
to \emph{your} setting \\
\end{tabularx}
Set 2--3 alerts; triage into Zotero weekly. Also
\lnk{https://scholar.google.com/scholar_labs/search}{Scholar Labs} (ask the full
question; it explains each hit)\dt \lnk{https://aclanthology.org}{ACL Anthology}\dt
\lnk{https://www.semanticscholar.org}{Semantic Scholar}\dt
\lnk{https://www.connectedpapers.com}{Connected Papers} (week one)\dt
\lnk{https://openreview.net}{OpenReview} (read the reviews)\dt
\lnk{https://paperswithcode.co}{paperswithcode.co}.
\sub{Read in 3 passes}5 min skim and decide $\to$ 1 h (figures, method) $\to$
hours (re-derive). Pass 3 for the five papers your thesis depends on.
\sub{Is this repo worth it?}last commit, contributors, who answers issues\dt
\textbf{search issues for ``reproduce''} before trusting its numbers\dt
checkpoints, pinned deps, \textbf{LICENSE}\dt stars measure marketing, not
correctness\dt citations per year $>$ raw totals.

%============================================================== COLUMN 3 ======
\head{AI assistant: honest use}
Assistant in the repo + a short memory file of your conventions\dt connect it
to your files and Zotero\dt dated \code{notes/}. Use for: unfamiliar notation,
getting someone's repo to run, boilerplate and plots, refactoring, LaTeX
errors, tightening prose you wrote, a hostile-examiner pass\dt a free first
review of your PDF from \lnk{https://research.google/deepreview/}{Deep Review} (two a
week; your supervisor still reads it).
\begin{flag}
\textbf{1 You must explain every line you submit} --- the defence tests exactly
this. \textbf{2} Never cite a paper you have not opened: fabricated references
fail theses. \textbf{3} Never let it write your results or your analysis.
\textbf{4} Follow your university's policy and declare use if required --- ask,
do not guess. \textbf{5} No confidential or personal data in a cloud tool.
\end{flag}

\columnbreak
\head{Doing the work}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Get the data; read \textbf{50 examples by hand}; count labels, find
        duplicates.
  \item Dumbest possible baseline (majority class, TF-IDF + logistic
        regression, a rule) --- this anchors everything.
  \item Reproduce the strong baseline.
  \item Only then your idea --- \textbf{one change at a time}, each measured.
  \item Ablate: remove each piece, show what it was worth. Start early.
  \item Error analysis: categorise 50--100 mistakes by hand.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{flag}
\textbf{Mistakes that cost marks:} test-set leakage (tuning on test,
duplicates, preprocessing fitted before the split)\dt one seed ($+0.4$ is
noise --- report mean $\pm$ std over 3+)\dt unfair comparison (your method
tuned a week, baseline on defaults)\dt accuracy on imbalanced data (use
macro-F1 / per class)\dt results you cannot regenerate\dt plots without axis
labels or a caption that states the conclusion.
\end{flag}
\sub{Results table from day one}date, commit, config, seed, dev score, note on
what changed. The results chapter is then formatting, not archaeology.
\textbf{Negative results are results} --- ``I tried X, it did not help, here is
the evidence and why'' is a legitimate finding.

\head{Writing the thesis}
Start in month one. Writing is how you find the hole in the story.
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{1.75cm}@{\hspace{3pt}}X@{\hspace{3pt}}>{\raggedleft\arraybackslash}p{0.72cm}@{}}
\rowcolor{rowtint}Abstract & problem, what you did, result & 1 \\
Introduction & question + contributions list & 3--5 \\
\rowcolor{rowtint}Background & only what the reader needs & 5--10 \\
Related work & by \emph{theme}, not one para per paper & 5--8 \\
\rowcolor{rowtint}Method & precise enough to reimplement & 5--10 \\
Experiments & data, splits, metrics, baselines & 8--15 \\
\rowcolor{rowtint}Analysis & ablations, errors, surprises & 4--8 \\
Conclusion & findings, limitations, future work & 2--3 \\
\end{tabularx}
\emph{Pages are a sane default --- your department's rules win.}
\sub{Advice}figures and tables first, then the text\dt each chapter opens with
what it does and closes with what it showed\dt past tense, active voice\dt
captions self-contained\dt cite properly, quote almost never --- copying
without quotes and a citation is plagiarism, and it is checked\dt give your
supervisor a \textbf{complete} draft 3 weeks before the deadline.

\head{The defence}
15--25 min + questions. Problem (2 slides) $\to$ why hard (1) $\to$ what you
did (3--5) $\to$ results (3--4) $\to$ analysis and limits (2) $\to$ conclusion.
Rehearse out loud, timed, twice\dt a 60-second demo, recorded as a fallback\dt
know your numbers (data sizes, your score, the baseline, training time).
\textbf{Expect:} why this baseline? what if the data changes? why does it fail
on class X? what with six more months? is the improvement significant?
``I do not know, but here is how I would find out'' is a fine answer.

\head{Six-month timetable}
\textbf{M1} topic + proposal signed off, tools installed, data inspected,
10--15 papers, trivial baseline. \textbf{M2} strong baseline reproduced,
logging, background + related work drafted. \textbf{M3} your method, first
results, method chapter. \textbf{M4} ablations and seeds, tables generated by
script. \textbf{M5} error analysis, \textbf{complete draft}, results frozen.
\textbf{M6} revisions, references checked one by one, slides, two rehearsals,
submit.
\begin{flag}
\textbf{Weekly, to your supervisor:} what I did\dt what I found\dt what is
blocked\dt what I propose\dt what I need from you. ``I am stuck'' sent on
Tuesday is worth three weeks of silence.
\end{flag}

\head{After the thesis}
Publish it if it is good (system paper, workshop, student research
workshop)\dt clean the repo: README that reproduces your main table, env file,
licence --- the best entry-level CV item\dt put up
\code{username.github.io}\dt ask for the reference letter while it is fresh\dt
thinking about a PhD? see the companion guide.
