%============================================================== COLUMN 1 ======
\head{The bar}
Between an undergraduate project and a PhD chapter: a \textbf{contribution},
but a bounded one, finished in two semesters. Expected beyond the undergrad
standard: a \emph{positioned} related-work chapter, ablations, several seeds,
significance where claimed, and an output that could become a workshop paper
with a month's work.
\begin{flag}
\textbf{The one-claim test.} \emph{For \textbf{[setting]}, \textbf{[what you
did]} achieves \textbf{[effect]} compared with \textbf{[baseline]}, because
\textbf{[mechanism]} --- shown by \textbf{[the experiment]}.} Cannot fill every
slot by month two? You have a project, not a thesis. The empty slot is your
next task --- usually \emph{because} (numbers, no explanation) or
\emph{compared with} (no honest baseline).
\end{flag}

\head{Finding a contribution}
Pick a question that is \textbf{open}, \textbf{answerable with your compute},
and \textbf{interesting whichever way it falls} --- then a failure is still a
finding.
\sub{1 Extend one paper}Most reliable. Push \emph{one} dimension: new language
or domain, distribution shift, a tenth of the compute or labels, longer
inputs, different evaluation. The contribution is where it breaks and why.
\sub{2 Reproduce and interrogate}Rebuild a published result, then ask what the
authors did not: other seeds, datasets, scales, prompts.
\sub{3 Shared task with an angle}\lnk{https://semeval.github.io}{SemEval} and
workshop tasks give data, metric, leaderboard. The thesis is the analysis of
\emph{why} systems place where they do.
\sub{4 Applied / industry}A real problem --- read the NDA column first.
\sub{5 Analysis or theory}Probing, robustness, efficiency measured honestly, a
metric critique with a small human evaluation, a bounded proof.
\sub{Where gaps are written down}Limitations sections\dt task overview
papers\dt survey taxonomies (thin boxes)\dt workshop CFPs\dt plateaued
leaderboards\dt \textbf{your supervisor's grant} (funded, data exists,
attentive supervision).

\head{Two-page proposal, before any code}
Question\dt \textbf{claim} (the sentence above)\dt why now (2--3 refs)\dt data
(name, size, licence, in hand?)\dt baselines (trivial + published + source of
its numbers)\dt method (3 bullets + the knob you vary)\dt evaluation (metric,
splits, seeds, significance test)\dt \textbf{compute budget}\dt milestones
(data, baseline, first result, freeze, draft)\dt risks\dt \textbf{fallback}
(the thesis you still have if the idea fails).
\begin{flag}
\textbf{Scope sinks theses, not difficulty.} Can you name the dataset and
metric today? Run one baseline end to end in three weeks? If the idea fails
completely, is there still a thesis? Any ``no'' --- narrow it now.
\end{flag}

%============================================================== COLUMN 2 ======
\head{Industry / NDA theses}
Settle \textbf{in writing, before you start}, with your academic supervisor in
the room: can the thesis be published and defended openly\dt is there an
embargo (6--12 months is common)\dt what may appear (method yes; raw data
often no; relative results usually negotiable when absolutes are not)\dt who
owns code and IP, and what may go on GitHub\dt who supervises what (degree
requirements win)\dt \textbf{fallback if access is withdrawn}.
\sub{Writing around confidential data}Describe data by statistics and
structure, not content, and reproduce the core experiment on a public dataset
so an examiner can check your reasoning. Do it from the start. GDPR and ethics
approval: ask in week one, it takes weeks.

\head{Related work: the chapter that separates degrees}
Undergrad summarises; master's \textbf{positions}. By theme, not by paper,
with a taxonomy figure, ending where nobody has been.
\begin{itemize}
  \item 5--10 anchor papers from your supervisor; steal their vocabulary.
  \item Same queries across Scholar, Semantic Scholar, Anthology, OpenReview,
        DBLP --- \textbf{log query, date, hits}.
  \item Snowball backward (references) + forward (cited-by, \emph{search
        within citing articles}).
  \item Screen 200--400, keep 60--100; extract into a table.
  \item Cluster rows until groups stop changing $=$ your taxonomy.
\end{itemize}
Read in 3 passes: 5 min decide $\to$ 1 h figures+method $\to$ hours for the
five papers the thesis depends on.

\lnk{https://www.zotero.org}{Zotero} + connector +

\lnk{https://aclanthology.org}{Anthology} / \lnk{https://dblp.org}{DBLP} or

sorted in week one\dt track every run (config, metrics, commit).

%============================================================== COLUMN 3 ======
\head{Evidence that survives an examiner}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Read 50 examples by hand; look for leakage between splits.
  \item \textbf{Trivial baseline} first --- it anchors everything.
  \item \textbf{Reproduce the published baseline}; if you cannot, finding out
        why is itself a result.
  \item Your method, one change at a time, each measured.
  \item \textbf{Ablate} --- start early, they always take longer.
  \item Error analysis, 50--100 cases by hand $=$ your \emph{because}.
\end{enumerate}
\sub{Claiming a difference}3+ seeds, \textbf{mean $\pm$ std}\dt
\textbf{paired bootstrap or permutation test} on test predictions\dt many
systems on one dataset inflates false positives --- say so\dt report compute
and the size of the hyperparameter search (a win after 100 trials against a
baseline tuned once is not a win)\dt splits fixed before you look; test set
touched once.
\begin{flag}
\textbf{Costs a grade:} leakage\dt unfair tuning\dt one seed\dt accuracy on
imbalanced data\dt a method chapter nobody could reimplement\dt a related-work
chapter that is a list\dt writing started in the last month\dt results you
cannot regenerate.
\end{flag}
\textbf{Negative results count} --- if the comparison was fair and you verified
your implementation reproduces a known number first.

\columnbreak
\head{AI assistant: five rules}
\begin{flag}
\textbf{1} Explain every line you submit --- the defence tests exactly this.
\textbf{2} Never cite a paper you have not opened. \textbf{3} Never let it
write your results or analysis. \textbf{4} Follow your university's policy and
declare use. \textbf{5} Nothing confidential into a cloud tool.
\end{flag}

\head{Writing \& supervision}
Start month one. Chapters: abstract 1\dt intro 4--6 (contributions as a
numbered list)\dt background 6--12\dt related work 8--12\dt method 8--12\dt
experiments 10--18\dt analysis 6--10\dt conclusion 2--4. Figures and tables
first, then the text. Captions self-contained. Limitations honest. Complete
draft to your supervisor \textbf{three weeks} before the deadline.
\sub{Supervision}Decide yourself: libraries, code structure, plots\dt bring to
the meeting: anything that changes the claim or scope\dt \textbf{escalate
immediately}: lost data or compute, ethics/NDA trouble, a deadline you will
miss. Five bullets the evening before: did\,/\,found\,/\,blocked\,/\,propose\,/\,need.

\head{Into a paper}
Plan it in month five, not after the defence. Cut background and most related
work; keep claim, method, key experiments, analysis. Target a workshop,
\textbf{Findings}, the Student Research Workshop, or a system paper. Agree
authorship early. Submit after the thesis, using examiner comments as free
reviews. Release code + checkpoints + \code{CITATION.cff}.

\head{The defence}
20--30 min + questions, before a committee that has read unevenly. Problem
(2 slides) $\to$ why it is hard + what exists (2--3) $\to$ \textbf{your claim}
(1) $\to$ method (3--5) $\to$ results (4--5) $\to$ analysis + limitations
(2--3) $\to$ conclusion. One idea per slide. Rehearse out loud, timed, twice.
Know your numbers: data sizes, your result, the baseline, spread across seeds,
training cost. \textbf{Expect:} why this baseline? is it significant? what
under distribution shift? why does it fail on class X? six more months?
``I do not know, but here is how I would find out'' is a fine answer; guessing
is not.

\head{What it becomes next}
\sub{A PhD application}The thesis is your evidence that you can do research.
Give referees the abstract, results and plans --- ask while it is fresh. A
workshop paper moves you from ``promising'' to ``has published''.
\sub{A job}The repository is the portfolio: a README that reproduces your main
table, honest limitations, clean code. Interviews ask what examiners ask.
\sub{Both}Not exclusive --- an industry year before a PhD is a good path.

\head{Nine months}
\textbf{M1} proposal signed, data in hand, 15--20 papers, trivial baseline.
\textbf{M2} published baseline reproduced, tracking, taxonomy drafted.
\textbf{M3--4} method, first results, claim sentence complete.
\textbf{M5} ablations, seeds, significance, publication plan.
\textbf{M6--7} error analysis, results frozen, tables by script.
\textbf{M8} \textbf{complete draft}, artefacts released.
\textbf{M9} revisions, citations checked, two rehearsals, submit.
